\subsection{Robustness and baseline comparison}
Across the 12 synthetic conditions where the actual Byzantine count and honest coverage satisfy the configured budgets ($f$ and $r$), the robust feasible-set rule achieved zero wrong decisive classifications (0\% FA and 0\% FR) with a mean decisive coverage of 87.52\%, outperforming the fixed-margin baseline (75.63\%) by 11.89 percentage points.



Table~\ref{tab:semantic} details the focus condition (correlated calibrated errors, maximum radius 4, and one Byzantine issuer). The robust rule achieves 85.04\% decisive coverage with zero errors, whereas the fixed-margin rule reaches 75.67\%. Point-estimation baselines (median point and majority center) classify 100\% of events but suffer from 2.10\% FA and 15.29\%--16.30\% FR due to boundary uncertainty. All-source intersection fails on 87.63\% of events and still incurs 8.64\% FA and 47.55\% FR when a Byzantine interval manipulates the remaining overlap. Using a 10,000-resample paired bootstrap across the 30 seed groups, the robust rule improves decisive coverage over fixed margins by 9.38 percentage points (95\% CI: $[8.25, 10.54]$ percentage points).

Grouping events by the actual honest-miscoverage count $M$ directly validates Proposition 1. In the focus condition, all 2,400 events satisfy $M = 0 \le r$ with zero wrong decisive outcomes (yielding a one-sided 95\% exact binomial upper bound of 9.50\% on the seed-group failure probability across the 30 independent seeds). Under the undercovered error model, the proportion of events satisfying $M \le r$ increases from 52.42\% ($r = 0$) to 62.79\% ($r = 1$) and 73.42\% ($r = 2$). Across all three settings, events satisfying $M \le r$ incurred strictly zero wrong decisive classifications, whereas premise violations ($M > r$) resulted in wrong-decisive rates of 1.31\%, 0.67\%, and 0.47\%, respectively.

\subsection{Ablation and out-of-budget stress tests}
Table 7 evaluates system behavior when external assumptions are violated. Under honest-source undercoverage ($M > r$) with one Byzantine issuer, the robust rule experiences 0.23\% FA and 3.41\% FR. When two Byzantine issuers attack a verifier configured for $f = 1$, evidence failure rises to 83.33\% and conditional FA/FR reach 11.62\% and 60.86\%, confirming that the Inconsistent state ($\mathcal{F}_k = \emptyset$) reflects empty threshold support rather than serving as a universal detector for out-of-budget collusion.

Table~\ref{tab:miscoverage} and Figure~\ref{fig:budget} show the paired ablation over the honest-miscoverage budget $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ for $n = 5, f = 1$. Because increasing $r$ lowers the support threshold $k = n - f - r$ and expands $\mathcal{F}_k$, it trades decisive coverage for stronger protection against uncalibrated honest misses. Under calibrated evidence, increasing $r$ from 0 to 2 reduces decisive coverage from 85.04\% to 77.46\% while maintaining zero errors. Under undercovered evidence, raising $r$ from 0 to 2 eliminates FA (from 0.23\% to 0\%) and reduces FR from 3.41\% to 0.94\%, with decisive coverage shifting from 84.92\% to 78.50\%.

Trajectory replay over the measured UrbanNav path corroborates these findings. Under nominal simulated errors, the robust rule achieves 93.27\% decisive coverage with zero errors, outperforming fixed margins (81.94\%) and avoiding the errors of the median-point baseline (0.29\% FA, 3.12\% FR). Under the $4\times$ common-mode bias stress test, the robust rule maintains low error rates of 0.032\% FA and 0.493\% FR.
