The studies in Table~\ref{tab:comparison-context} share important ingredients, but they organize them around different verification objects. In particular, SPOON already combines anonymous reporting and double-report identification, while TST-Audit already specifies a credential-linked, grid--epoch-scoped microproof. Thus, anonymity or a scoped tag alone is not a distinguishing contribution here. Bogdanov et al. already use signed location claims in privacy-preserving vehicle-policy verification, and Brito et al. examine content provenance using independent witnesses. The contribution evaluated in this paper is the specific report-acceptance relation that ties a credential secret, an issuer-signed event and the received report envelope to a task-scoped tag, together with its executable extensions and controlled substitution tests. This characterization does not imply that an alternative instantiation of an earlier interface cannot provide a similar relation.
