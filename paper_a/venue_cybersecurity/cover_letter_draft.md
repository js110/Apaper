Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our Research manuscript, “Zero-Knowledge Certification of Robust Spacetime Compliance under Byzantine Evidence for Vehicular Crowdsensing,” for publication in *Cybersecurity*.

This work addresses a privacy and integrity gap in vehicular crowdsensing: existing zero-knowledge location predicates do not, by themselves, certify that an event is supported by multiple authenticated but uncertain sources under a defined Byzantine budget. We establish a four-valued certification relation over signed spacetime uncertainty boxes and implement it using two Groth16/BN254 circuits with report-bound gateway verification. Our evaluation separates cryptographic correctness from physical source-coverage assumptions, combines protocol-level adversarial checks with synthetic semantic studies, and measures the cost of proving the authenticated relation.

The manuscript fits the journal's coverage of applied cryptography, privacy-enhancing technologies, IoT security and adversarial reasoning. We make no claim of validating real multisource vehicle-sensor calibration; source independence and online calibration remain outside the certified relation.
